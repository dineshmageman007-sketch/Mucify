How it works

Mucify turns a Spotify playlist into lossless FLAC files in four steps. Run them in order, or use only the ones you need.

  1. 1
    Playlist ManagerRemembers the songs you already have and compares each new playlist against them, so only new songs move on.
  2. 2
    Qobuz EnrichCleans up the names and looks up each song's official best quality (bit depth and sample rate). Optional: without Qobuz, every song uses 24-bit / 48 kHz as its limit.
  3. 3
    DownloaderSearches Soulseek for every song and downloads the best matching FLAC.
  4. 4
    Post-ProcessingTags your FLAC library with ReplayGain 2.0 so all songs play at the same loudness.

Need the starting CSV? Export your Spotify playlist with chosic.com's Spotify Playlist Exporter.

Why a Playlist Manager?

Playlists overlap a lot. Without a memory of what you already own, every new playlist would download the same songs again.

  • It keeps a library of the songs you have already downloaded. Songs are recognised by their Spotify track ID, so different spellings or extra words in a title don't fool it.
  • When you compare a new playlist, it is split into already have and to download. Only the second list goes on to the next steps, and it is saved for you automatically.
  • After you finish a playlist, drop its CSV into the library box so those songs are skipped next time.
  • Backup saves your whole library as one CSV. You can drop that file back in later to restore it.

Where your files are saved

Your music (FLAC files)…Every downloaded song lands here.
Playlist lists (CSV files)…One folder per playlist, filled in automatically: 1_to_download.csv (songs you don't have yet), 2_enriched.csv (with Qobuz quality), 3_skipped.csv (everything that did not download) and 4_successful.csv (everything that did).
Settings, logins and logs…A hidden Windows folder, kept private on your PC. Settings → Reset & uninstall clears it.

Tip: feed 3_skipped.csv back into the Downloader to retry only the songs that didn't download.

Why Mucify

Mucify is one small installer: no Docker, no WSL, no Python, no command line and no browser tab. Under the hood it is careful about what it accepts:

  • FLAC only, never lower. Only FLAC files are accepted. Once the first file of a song is chosen, every retry must match or beat its bit depth and sample rate.
  • The right song. The title must be in the file name, the length must be within 2 seconds of the original, and remix, live, edit and extended versions are skipped unless the title asks for them.
  • The best file. Of the matches, it picks the biggest file (the most complete FLAC), and the fastest uploader if sizes tie.
  • No getting stuck. A download that is too slow (under 3 MB after 30 seconds) is dropped and the next source is tried, up to 15 attempts per song.
  • No repeat work. Songs you already own are skipped, and anything that failed is kept in 3_skipped.csv for another go.
  • Private. No accounts, no ads, no tracking. Everything stays on your PC.

Mucify can only find what other Soulseek users share, so a few songs may not be available. They end up in 3_skipped.csv.

Good to know

  • Qobuz is optional. Sign in with an email and password (Google, Apple and Facebook sign-in don't work in Mucify's window). If the connection expires, a banner asks you to reconnect.
  • Soulseek login. Any username and password work: a new username is created the first time you connect. Use Test connection in Settings to check it.
  • Background music and mini player. Pick a file or a whole folder in Settings. The player in the sidebar lets you skip around.
  • Fullscreen. Press F11. Mucify remembers its size and position, but always starts windowed or maximized, never fullscreen.
